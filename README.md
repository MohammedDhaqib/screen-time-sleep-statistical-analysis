# screen-time-sleep-statistical-analysis
Statistical analysis of screen time, sleep quality, digital eye strain, and self-reported hair loss among 150 college students using chi-square tests, Spearman correlation, and multiple linear regression.

Association of Screen Time and Sleep Quality with Digital Eye Strain and Self-Reported Hair Loss Among College Students

📌 Overview

This project investigates the association between screen time, sleep duration, digital eye strain, and self-reported hair loss among college students. The study uses statistical methods to explore patterns and relationships in data collected from 150 respondents.

The project demonstrates the application of statistical analysis to real-world student lifestyle and health-related data.

🎯 Objectives

- Analyze daily screen time and sleep duration among college students.
- Examine the relationship between screen time and digital eye strain.
- Investigate the association between sleep duration and self-reported hair loss.
- Apply statistical techniques to identify patterns and relationships in the dataset.
- Interpret findings while acknowledging the limitations of self-reported data.

📊 Dataset

- Sample size: 150 respondents
- Study design: Cross-sectional study
- Study population: College students
- Data type: Self-reported questionnaire data

The dataset contains information related to screen time, sleep duration, digital eye strain, hair loss, and other respondent characteristics.

🛠️ Statistical Methods

The following methods were used in the analysis:

- Descriptive Statistics: Frequencies and percentages to summarize respondent characteristics and responses.
- Pearson's Chi-Square Test: To examine associations between categorical variables.
- Spearman's Rank Correlation: To assess relationships between ranked or ordinal variables.
- Exploratory Linear Regression: To investigate the relationships between selected predictors and outcomes.
- Robust Standard Errors (HC3): To account for potential heteroscedasticity in regression inference.

🔍 Key Findings

- Screen time: 40% of respondents reported 4–6 hours of daily screen time, while 13.3% reported more than 8 hours.
- Sleep duration: 42.7% reported sleeping for 6–7 hours per day.
- Digital eye strain: 56% reported experiencing eye strain often.
- Self-reported hair loss: 34.7% reported moderate hair loss, while 24% reported severe hair loss.
- Screen time and eye strain: A positive Spearman correlation was observed ((\rho = 0.504,\ p < 0.001)).
- Sleep duration and hair loss: A weak negative Spearman correlation was observed ((\rho = -0.183,\ p = 0.025)).
- Screen time and hair loss: No statistically significant Spearman correlation was observed ((\rho = 0.024,\ p = 0.770)).

💡 Conclusion

The analysis identified a notable association between screen time and self-reported digital eye strain. Sleep duration showed a weak negative association with self-reported hair loss, whereas screen time did not show a statistically significant correlation with hair loss in this sample.

These findings describe associations within the study sample and do not establish cause-and-effect relationships.

 Limitations

- The study uses a cross-sectional design, so causal relationships cannot be established.
- The data are self-reported and may be subject to recall or reporting bias.
- The findings may not generalize to all college students.
- Some categorical statistical tests had sparse expected cell counts, which may affect the reliability of their results.
- Data consistency should be verified before further analysis or publication.

 Future Scope

- Collect data from a larger and more diverse student population.
- Use validated questionnaires to assess sleep quality and digital eye strain.
- Include additional variables such as stress, diet, physical activity, and academic workload.
- Conduct longitudinal studies to investigate changes over time.
- Explore further statistical models after checking their assumptions.
